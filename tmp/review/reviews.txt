Reviewer:
This work aims to identify inward-propagating compressive fluctuations using coordinated radio observations of Tianwen-1 from multiple ground stations. The authors argue that these inward-propagating fluctuations coexist with outward-propagating disturbances and are generated through reflection associated with a localized density enhancement. They further suggest that the observations support a reflection-driven wave scenario and that the observed phase fluctuations are qualitatively consistent with their one-dimensional MHD simulations.
The topic is interesting, but the current form of the manuscript is difficult to follow and many key aspects of the analysis and physical interpretation are not sufficiently explained. In particular, the role of the multi-station observations is not clearly demonstrated. Although data from multiple stations are presented, much of the analysis appears to rely on different pairs of stations identifying different inward-propagating features, rather than multiple stations independently observing and tracking the same structure. It is more like a collection of separate two-station correlation analyses.
Overall, the manuscript requires substantial revision before I can make a final evaluation. Many important details of the observations and physical meaning are left unclear. I strongly suggest the authors to reorganize and substantially rewrite the manuscript, and present the figures in a way that allows the reader to directly follow the statement in the main text. In its present form, I recommend major revision.

Lines 96: The authors state that eight sessions are within 10 R_sun, while the distances archived in Table 1 only show seven observations are within 10 R_sun.
Line 96 - 97: What do the authors mean by "most dynamically active"? Why do the authors expect they are more active within 10 R_sun? The authors need to elaborate this claim or some references may be needed here to demonstrate the "fine-scale" compressive fluctuations are more dynamically active "within 10 R_sun".
Line 100: The closet approach point is already a position in three-dimensional space. Why do the authors use "projected into Carrington coordinate" here? Do the authors mean "transform"?
Figure 1b,c: I'm a bit confused by the statements made by the authors from lines 99-103 and caption of figure 1. In line 99-101, the authors define the "projected baseline" in the Carrington coordinate system and cite Fig. 1b,c. While, in caption 1, the authors say "the dots indicate the station projections on the sky plane". In the last sentence of caption 1, the authors again define "projection baseline". The authors need to consistently state the coordinate system in which the "projected baseline/projection baseline" is defined and/or Fig. 1b&c show.
Figure 1b,c: Why do the authors chose to show two times lying outside the maximum observation interval listed in Table 1 for the corresponding dates?
Line 103: What do the authors mean by "second-level" here? Does is refer to "on the order of seconds"?
Line 147-148: It is hard to understand the formation of the double peaks from the explanation by the authors. First, what is the time resolution of the frequency time series? Secondly, if there's indeed coexisting outward and inward components, why not the outward peak show in the cross-correlation spectrum but rather an effective mean response is present? The authors chose a long interval lasting 26 minutes, I was wondering if the double-peak is a steady signature within this interval.
Line 146-148: The authors need to discuss the possible relation between the wavelet cross-correlation results and the traditional cross correlation results.
Figure 2: The authors estimate speeds from either the time differences of the peaks in frequency time series or the peaked lag in the cross-correlation spectra. The authors need to define them separately and explicitly in the main text.
Lines 143-146: This wavelet cross-correlation analysis lacks physical conclusion. What can lag and period tell us about the information in the near Sun region? The negative lags are observed at different times and periods, although with similar lags. Does this figure indicate that there exist compressible fluctuations over multiple scales? However, the authors only use one wavelength in their simulation.
Line 152: Why do the authors only show the wavelet analysis until 07:10 but not extend to later times where Js also have observations?
Lines 152-153: The authors say "Owing to their long and oblique projected separations, ....". The authors seem to contradict their use of Taylor's hypothesis. This statement makes me feel that the small-scale fluctuations will be decorrelated over separation length ~4000km. Particularly, the simulation generates waves of 94km's, consistent with -8 s lag inward waves, which all exhibit periods smaller than 500 s, as shown in Figure 2g.
Lines 154-155: I'm so confused by this claim made by the authors. Firstly, please define "consistent". The authors already pointed out their inward-propagating signatures are independently observed in Js-Bd and Bd-Hh. The inward signatures in Js-Bd wavelet result spans from periods of 64s to 256s, while that in Bd-Hh wavelet spectra is at ~1024 s. The times and the periods are both different in different cross-correlation analysis. That means these results do not show the same structures. However, in the last sentence, the authors claim "the inward propagating component is not a projection artifact, but a genuine sunward-propagating structure in the plane of the sky". I don't see a real utilization of multiple-station measurements to infer the propagation information of the same structures.
Lines 162-163: As I mentioned in 13, the authors didn't identify the same structure in the observations of the three stations. The authors cannot reach this conclusion.
Lines 160-161: The authors need to show the wavelet analysis of Yg-Hh, at least in the reply to the referee's report.
Line 170-171: v_fast and v_slow need definitions here.
Lines 189-191: Does the simulation show similar Fourier spectra as shown in Fig.3e?
Eq (1): The authors say \Delta \rho is the superposition of the fundamental mode and its harmonic. Where is the fundamental mode in this equation? Why is the symbol "-" in the first term? Why the fourth harmonic absent?
Lines 197-198: what is the fundamental frequency?
Line 236: What is m1a18x?
The authors provide a separate file listing the results of other observations. However, there's no citation to those results in the main text.
